Privacy

Last changed November 23, 2026

poetsheaf is an invitation-only place for poets to keep their poems, the journals they send to, and their submissions. Your poems are yours. This page says plainly what poetsheaf keeps, who can see it, and how to have it removed.

What poetsheaf keeps

What you give it:

  • Your email address, your name and the name you publish under.
  • Your poems, with their notes, dates, ratings and themes.
  • The journals and contests you follow, their reading periods, and your notes about them.
  • Your submissions and how they turned out, and your collections.
  • What you add for Word files and cover letters: contact details, a bio, and a section-break ornament if you choose one.
  • Poems and submission histories you bring in from your own Word, text or spreadsheet files. The files are read in your browser, and only what you choose to add is sent to poetsheaf, not the files themselves.

What it notes on its own:

  • Your time zone, as your browser reports it, so that “today” in poetsheaf is your day.
  • When you were invited, when you joined, and when you last signed in.
  • The services poetsheaf runs on keep short-lived technical logs, such as the IP address and browser behind each request, to keep the service secure and working.

Who can see it

  • Only you. The database itself keeps each poet’s records apart, so no other poet can see or change yours.
  • To manage invitations and accounts, poetsheaf’s administrator sees a list of poets: names, email addresses, and when each was invited, joined and last signed in. That list doesn’t include anyone’s poems or submissions.
  • Like the people who run any online service, the administrator can reach the database through the hosting provider’s tools. They don’t read your poems or records, except to fix a problem you have asked about.

What poetsheaf doesn’t do

  • It doesn’t sell or rent your information, or share it for advertising.
  • It doesn’t use your poems to train AI models, or for anything except showing them to you.
  • It has no ads, no analytics and no tracking cookies.
  • It claims no rights in your work.

Where it’s kept, and who helps

poetsheaf runs on a few services that handle your information only to provide poetsheaf:

  • Supabase holds the database, signs you in, and runs the connection to Claude. Your records are stored in the United States (Amazon Web Services, Oregon), encrypted on disk, and sent over encrypted connections.
  • GitHub Pages serves the poetsheaf.com pages. It never receives your poems or records, though like any web host it sees the addresses of the browsers that visit.
  • Resend sends poetsheaf’s emails, such as invitations and password resets, so it receives your email address. If you have a copy of your records emailed to you (every week, or when you ask), or if poetsheaf closes your account, Resend also carries that copy, and keeps its record of sent emails for 30 days.
  • Anthropic answers Ask Claude, only if you turn it on (see below).
  • Squarespace holds the poetsheaf.com name and forwards mail sent to poetsheaf addresses.

Your password is handled by the sign-in service and kept only in scrambled (hashed) form, so poetsheaf never sees it.

On your device

poetsheaf keeps you signed in, and remembers a few display choices such as how your lists are sorted and your Word file and printing options, in your browser’s local storage. Signing out ends the sign-in. Clearing your browser’s data for poetsheaf.com removes the rest.

Word files and PDFs for printing are made here in your browser, from what’s already on your screen: your poems aren’t sent anywhere to make them.

If you connect Claude

You can choose to connect poetsheaf to Claude, Anthropic’s AI assistant. You approve the connection on a poetsheaf page. Then, when you ask it to, Claude can read your poetsheaf records and add to or change them; it can’t delete anything. What Claude receives is covered by Anthropic’s privacy policy and your Claude settings. You can disconnect at any time under More → Claude, in “Use poetsheaf in Claude”.

If you use Ask Claude here

Ask Claude is off until you turn it on under More → Claude. When you ask it something, poetsheaf sends your question, and the records Claude looks up to answer it, to Anthropic, which makes Claude. Anthropic doesn’t use them to train its models and deletes them within 30 days. Claude can add to or change your records only after you say yes to each change, and it can’t delete anything. poetsheaf doesn’t keep the conversation; it keeps only how many questions you asked each day, so that a daily limit can hold. You can turn Ask Claude off at any time.

Keeping and deleting

  • Your records stay until you delete them or close your account. You can delete poems, submissions, journals and collections in poetsheaf at any time.
  • You can download a copy of everything you keep in poetsheaf whenever you like, as an Excel workbook: under More → Files, choose Download everything. You can also have the same workbook emailed to you, every Sunday morning (tick Email me a copy every week) or whenever you choose (Email me a copy now). It’s sent only to the address you sign in with, and poetsheaf keeps no copy of it.
  • You can close your account yourself: under More → Account, choose Close my account. With your password, it deletes your account and everything in it straight away. Or email privacy@poetsheaf.com from the address you sign in with, and your account and everything in it will be deleted within 30 days. Either way, copies in the hosting provider’s backups expire within a further 30 days.
  • If poetsheaf suspends your account under the terms of use, you’re told by email, with the reason. While it’s suspended you can’t sign in and Claude can’t reach it, but nothing in it is deleted or changed; if it’s restored, everything is as you left it.
  • If poetsheaf closes your account, whether you asked or under the terms of use, it first emails you a copy of everything in it, the same workbook as Download everything. Only then are the account and everything in it deleted. poetsheaf keeps no copy, and the administrator doesn’t see yours.
  • If you were invited but never joined, your email address is kept with the invitation until it’s cancelled. Ask, and it will be.
  • At the same address you can ask for a copy of everything poetsheaf holds about you, or for anything in it to be corrected.

Your rights

poetsheaf uses your information only to provide the service you joined. Depending on where you live, the law may give you rights over your personal information, such as to see it, correct it, take a copy, have it deleted, or object to how it’s used. poetsheaf honors these requests from anyone, wherever they live: write to privacy@poetsheaf.com. If you live in the European Union or the United Kingdom, you can also complain to your data protection authority.

Children

poetsheaf isn’t meant for anyone under 16, and doesn’t knowingly keep information about children.

Changes

If this page changes, the date at the top changes too. Poets will be told by email about any change that affects how their information is used.

Questions

Write to privacy@poetsheaf.com.
